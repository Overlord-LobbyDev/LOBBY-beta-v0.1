Lobby · Tournaments · Below the stage

Three ways to build the lower half

The stage and its rails are settled. This is only about what sits under them, and it has three jobs: start a tournament, see what you have running, and see what is coming — with the preview carrying the detail so the lists do not have to.

What is wrong now — 01

The lists are duplicated. Ongoing and Upcoming both show your brackets, then Your run shows the same cards a third time further down.

What is wrong now — 02

The preview is in the top-right rail, a long way from the lists that feed it. You pick on the left and the answer appears in the opposite corner.

What is wrong now — 03

Create is a slab. It has the widest column on the page and puts one button in it, while the two lists that need width are squeezed into 340px rails.

A · The desk · recommended B · Two lanes + detail strip C · Command column Verdict
A

The desk

Recommended

One list, not three. Ongoing and Upcoming become a filter rather than two separate columns, which kills the duplication in one move and gives every row the full width of the page to say something useful. The preview sits directly beside the list and sticks as you scroll, so choosing and reading happen in the same place. Create is a bar across the top of the list — always the first thing, never a slab.

What it buys

The duplication is gone. Rows get real width, so a row can carry the round rail, the fill and a countdown at once. Preview is next to the list and stays put while you scroll it.

Scales

Works the same at 3 tournaments and at 300. Nothing has to be redesigned when the list grows.

What it costs

You see one slice at a time. Ongoing and Upcoming are a click apart rather than both on screen — softened by the counts sitting in the filter itself.

B

Two lanes + detail strip

Keeps both lists visible at once, which is the one thing A gives up. Ongoing and Upcoming become two wide lanes of cards instead of two narrow rails of rows, create shrinks to a single band across the top with the game picker built into it, and the preview becomes a full-width strip underneath — so the critical info is laid out horizontally with room to breathe rather than stacked in a 390px column.

What it buys

Both lists on screen at once, and the preview gets the full width — art, progress, entrants, details and actions all in one line instead of a tall stack.

Create feels like starting

The game picker is in the band, so the first choice happens before you have opened anything.

What it costs

The preview is below the lists, so picking something moves your eye down and back. Needs a scroll-into-view, and on a short window it sits off screen.

C

Command column

Splits the section by what it is for rather than by tournament state. A narrow sticky column on the left is everything you do — create, join by code, and your own next match with its countdown. Everything else is a browsing grid on the right with the state as tabs. The preview opens as a sheet over the grid, so it can be large without owning permanent space.

What it buys

The things you act on never scroll away, and the countdown to your own next match gets to be the biggest number on the page.

Browsing gets real room

Three cards across instead of one narrow rail, so the public list stops feeling like an afterthought.

What it costs

A sheet hides what is behind it, and it is the most work to build: open and close states, focus handling, and a second place the preview has to render.

Where I would go

A, with one borrowed piece from C. A is the only one of the three that removes the duplication rather than rearranging it, and having the preview stick beside the list is the difference between reading and hunting. The piece worth taking from C is the next-match countdown — that is the single most useful thing on the page for somebody already in a bracket, and in A it can sit in the toolbar beside Create.

B is the right answer only if seeing Ongoing and Upcoming simultaneously matters more than everything else. It reads well in a mockup with six tournaments and gets thin at sixty, because two half-width lanes cannot grow.
