Six ways to make it one with the app

Nothing here moves anything. Same columns, same order, same placement — these are all surface and depth. Each pair below is the real CSS of both treatments side by side, so what you are looking at is what the page would actually do, not a picture of it.

01

The whole top band bleeds

Biggest change

The left rail already runs off the glass, but the picture and the calendar still sit in boxes with air around them — so the band reads as one anchored thing and two floating ones. If all three run to the top and to their own edges with no gaps between, the entire top of the page becomes a single surface that the stream is set into.

What it buys

The top of the page stops being three objects and becomes one surface. It is the single biggest step toward "embedded".

What it costs

The picture loses its floating frame, so the stream meets the calendar with only a seam between them. Worth seeing at full size before committing.

02

Ambient tint from what is playing

Cheap

The page is the same colour whatever is on. Pull the dominant colour out of the current broadcast's thumbnail and let a very low wash of it sit behind the top band, so the page shifts with the stream. Discover already does exactly this on its cards with --dc-art, so the pattern and the plumbing both exist — it is one custom property and a gradient.

03

Depth instead of hairlines

The bar, the top row and the breaker each draw their own 1px line. Lines are how a document separates things; a surface separates them with shadow and space. Same regions, same order — the divider is a soft shadow cast by the region above rather than a drawn edge.

04

A little grain on the ground

Cheap

Flat dark UI reads as plastic because nothing in it has a texture. Two or three percent of monochrome noise across the page ground reads as a screen rather than a fill, and it makes the wells look deeper for free because the grain sits on the ground and not in them. One generated image, no asset, no request.

05

What is playing reaches further out

The live row is marked with a border colour, which is the same way everything else is marked. Since the rail is anchored to the glass, the current broadcast can say so with position: it reaches a little further into the edge than its neighbours and carries a wash of the accent. Selection becomes something the layout does rather than something a colour announces.

06

The page seats into the window

Cheap

The overlay meets the app chrome with hard corners, so it reads as a sheet dropped over the window. Rounding only the top two corners of the scroll area seats it into the chrome the way a panel does — the same detail that makes the sidebar and the chat feel built in rather than layered on.

What I would take

02 and 01, in that order. The ambient tint is the most feel for the least code and it reuses a pattern the app already has, so it cannot look foreign. The single band is the bigger move and the one that actually answers "embedded", but it is worth looking at full size first because the stream ends up meeting the calendar with only a seam.

04 with them if you like it — grain is nearly free and it flatters the wells. 06 only if we do every hub, otherwise Tournaments looks like a mistake. 03 I would hold: you asked for more obvious breakers a few rounds ago and this pulls against that, so it is probably right between the big regions and wrong for the section heads. 05 is a taste call and I would do it last.
