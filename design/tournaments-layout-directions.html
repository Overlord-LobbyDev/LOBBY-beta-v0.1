Three ways to lay out Tournaments

You said it doesn't pop, wastes the cards, wastes the bracket structure, ignores the real-world majors, and sits too centred. Here are three layouts that each fix all five — they differ in where your own match lives and what the page leads with. Pick one and I'll build it.

Locked, whichever you pick

  • Public, or private by code. Its own code type — 4 letters + 4 digits, EVOX-4417 — not reused from anywhere else in the app.
  • Completely separate from Lobby tournaments. Those stay private to their Lobby and never appear here.
  • Leads with you. The tournament you're in, your next match, a countdown, and standings you can read without entering.
  • Majors rail is curated. Evo, CEO, ECW and championship finals, read from a server table you populate — follow and countdown only, never entered here.
  • Auto-rotation. One live major takes the stage; several rotate.
  • Create a tournament is a first-class action on the page, not buried in a menu.

Reads best when

It's the clearest information design of the three: one glance tells you what's running and what you can join, and nothing competes for the same space. The brackets are the content rather than a preview panel, so the structure you said we're wasting is the whole left half of the page. Create and join-by-code get a permanent home.

What it costs

Cards shrink to fit half a page, so the art pipeline does less work — this is the least "poster" of the three. And with only one or two tournaments running, the left column looks thin next to the right.

What I'd pick, and why

Recommendation

The Marquee — because your first complaint was that nothing pops, and it's the only one of the three that solves that in its structure rather than its styling. It also does the most with your two strongest assets: the rotating stage is exactly where a live major belongs, and the staggered bands are the "left and right diversity" you asked for, in a way the two-rail layouts can't be.

The honest trade is filters. If you browse tournaments the way you browse Discover — heavy filtering, lots of comparing — The Desk is the better tool and the more consistent app. If the page is somewhere you check what's on and then act, The Marquee wins.

The Split is the one I'd hold in reserve. It's the best pure information design here, but it's the weakest at what you actually asked for first.

Whichever you pick, the bracket, the countdown, the standings preview, the majors rotation, create, and join-by-code all ship — they're placed differently, not cut.
