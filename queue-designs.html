Lobby · Queue

Three ways to make
Queue a moment

The glass version was decoration — aurora, frost and grain doing work the content should be doing. Home isn't beautiful because of effects. It has none. It's beautiful because one piece of game art is enormous, everything else recedes, and the whole page takes its colour from whatever you're looking at.

All three below steal exactly that. Click the cards — they're live.

Direction A

The Deck

Queue's picker becomes Home's deck — the same geometry, the same recede-and-dim, the same wash. You already love this interaction; the argument is that the game you're about to queue for deserves it more than a game you played last week. Setup collapses to one bar underneath, because once the deck is the hero nothing else should compete.

Direction B

The Marquee

Full-bleed library hero art — the wide cinematic key art Steam ships, which Lobby already fetches and currently only uses as a blurred wash. The game fills the screen like a launcher's play screen; your setup and the live numbers sit on top of it. The library becomes a filmstrip along the bottom.

Direction C

The Table

The one that's actually about meeting strangers rather than about picking a game. Empty seats around a table, filling in real time as people arrive. The wait stops being a spinner and becomes a thing you watch happen — and it's the only direction here that has an obvious NEXT once you're seated.

My call: A for the choosing, C for the waiting. They're not competitors — A is the screen before you press the button and C is the screen after. B is the most striking single image but it's a storefront, and Queue isn't selling you a game.
